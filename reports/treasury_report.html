Cash Forecast Report

Prepared for the Treasury · 4 August 2026

No forecast is being published today. The forecasting work is part-way through a rebuild, and we are not willing to put numbers in front of you that we cannot yet stand behind.

What happened

A detailed review found that the way we were cleaning the daily data was using information from 2025 to tidy up records from earlier years. That is not allowed: it makes the forecasts look better than they really are, because the model has effectively seen part of the answer.

We have fixed the cleaning. The immediate effect is that the yardstick we measure forecasts against turns out to be considerably harder than we previously reported — about 37% harder. Every accuracy figure quoted before this fix was too flattering, and all of them are being recalculated.

What is still to do before we can report

  • Put all four modelling approaches on the same measuring stick. At present one of them forecasts five calendar days ahead while the others forecast five working days ahead, so their results are not comparable.
  • Rebuild the forecasting models against the corrected data.
  • Check the honesty of the prediction ranges. On the current models, a range advertised as covering 8 days in 10 was measured covering only about 5 days in 10 on the busiest days — which is exactly when a cash buffer matters most.
  • Test the result once, on a year of data the models have never seen, and report that number without adjustment.

What we are not doing

We are not lowering any of the quality thresholds to let a model through. Where a model fails a check, it will be reported as withheld, with the reason in plain language, alongside the models that pass.

Scope of this work. Three series are in scope: Revenues, Expenditure, and the State budget balance. Earlier reports covered Revenues only — one of the 41 series in the source workbook — and should not be read as covering the budget as a whole. All amounts, when reported, will be in Georgian lari.
